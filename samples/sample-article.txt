Riverside Library to Open on Sundays From November

The Riverside town library will open on Sundays from 1 November, ending a closure that has lasted since 2009, the town council announced on Tuesday.

The new hours run from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. and apply to the main library only. The two smaller branch libraries will keep their present hours. Council officials said the change follows a survey in which 1,240 residents took part, and 62 percent asked for weekend opening.

The extra day will cost about 38,000 dollars a year, mainly for two part-time staff members and heating. The council said it will pay for the first year from its existing library budget and review the cost before deciding whether to continue. "We cannot promise it will last beyond next year," said council member Anita Verma, "but the demand is clear."

Library director Samuel Okafor said Sunday visitors are likely to be families and students. He expects the reading room and the computer area to be busiest, and he plans to hold a children's story hour on the first Sunday of each month.

Not everyone welcomed the decision. A local taxpayers' group said the money could be better spent on repairing the library roof, which has leaked twice this year. The council replied that roof repairs are already planned for spring and are funded separately.

Staff will be asked, not required, to work on Sundays, and those who agree will be paid at a higher rate. Volunteers from the Friends of the Library group have offered to help at the front desk during the first month.

The library will publish the full Sunday timetable on its website next week.
